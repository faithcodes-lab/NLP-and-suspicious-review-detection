

This project was one of the most challenging yet rewarding experiences of my academic journey so far. At various points it felt genuinely overwhelming, not just because of the technical complexity involved, but because of the human side of the work. Communication barriers within the team and contribution discrepancies made certain phases mentally draining, and there were moments where it felt like I was carrying more responsibility than I should have been.

Looking back, however, I recognise that those difficult moments were also some of the most formative ones. Taking on the role of team leader for a project of this scale pushed me in ways I did not anticipate. I had to learn how to assign tasks fairly, follow up on progress, give and receive feedback constructively, and find ways to bring team members along even when we were not moving at the same pace. These are not skills that can be learned from a textbook, they come from being placed in real situations with real stakes, and having to navigate them. I came to understand that if leadership is struggling, the whole team feels it. That awareness made me more disciplined and more proactive, even in the moments when I was struggling myself.

In practice, this meant taking ownership of a share of the technical work from the data engineering and pipeline setup, through model development and evaluation, to building and deploying the web application. As a team leader, I often found myself stepping in across multiple areas to ensure the project stayed on track, which while demanding, ultimately deepened my technical breadth and reinforced my ability to work under pressure.

From a technical standpoint, I entered this project with limited experience in machine learning, and the depth of what I have learned has surprised me. In the early stages of the project, I researced how to handle a dataset of over 233 million rows, something I had never encountered before. Through research I discovered DuckDB and learned how to connect to the database, convert a large CSV file to Parquet format, which reduced the file size from 87GB to 46GB and made it far more manageable to work with. Using the combination of DuckDB and Python, I moved through the full data science pipeline, data exploration, cleaning, and extracting meaningful insights that informed the preprocessing decisions. Importantly, this was not just about cleaning data, but cleaning data in a way that a machine learning model could learn effectively from.

From there I worked through model training and evaluation for both sentiment analysis and suspicious review detection, building comparison notebooks to evaluate multiple models side by side. Through my research I discovered the value of feature ablation, a method I had not previously encountered which proved essential in identifying which features the model was genuinely learning from and which were adding noise. This directly shaped the final model design.

One of the most significant engineering challenges I worked through was the training-serving skew problem. During training, review frequency was computed from the full dataset, but when the model was deployed to the web application, there was no way for it to know a new reviewer's posting history. Through research I discovered how the industry handles this at scale using feature stores, and for a project of our size I adapted this into a reviewer lookup table, a practical and evidence backed solution that ensured the model still received a meaningful input at inference time.

Building and deploying the web application was another layer of learning entirely. The process of deploying, testing, identifying issues, going back into the code, and pushing updates gave me a genuine appreciation for how software developers work iteratively in the real world. Each update taught me something new about how machine learning systems behave in production versus in a notebook.

One of the most unexpected areas of growth was learning how to communicate complex technical insights to people with significantly more experience than me. Presenting findings to supervisor and receiving critical feedback pushed me to develop not just technical confidence but communicative confidence, being able to explain what I built, why I built it that way, and what the results mean in a way that is clear and defensible. This mirrors the demands of the professional world, where data scientists must regularly communicate insights to senior stakeholders who will challenge assumptions and push for clarity.

There were moments early on where I doubted whether I had the capability to see this project through. Completing it has shown me that I can handle complex, multiple stage technical work even under pressure, even when things go wrong, and even when I have to carry the team forward at the same time. My technical capabilities as a data scientist have evolved significantly over the course of this project, and I am genuinely grateful for everything it has taught me as I move forward in my career.

## 5. Final Project Deliverable

The team presented the intended final output of the project.

The final deliverable will be a **Streamlit web application** that demonstrates the suspicious review detection system.

The application will allow users to:

- Input a product review  
- Receive a **sentiment prediction**

The goal of the interface is to present the modelling results in a clear and interactive way.

## 6. Research Contributions and Insights

One example of useful research explored by the team involved studies where researchers **generated fake reviews intentionally** in order to evaluate detection methods.

In the study discussed:

- Fake reviews were generated using AI tools such as ChatGPT.  
- Multiple algorithms were used to classify reviews as fake or genuine.  
- Human evaluators were also involved in judging the authenticity of the reviews.

This approach was useful because when researchers generate the fake reviews themselves, they can be certain about which reviews are fraudulent.

The team found this insight valuable because real-world datasets often do not contain confirmed labels for fake reviews.

## 8. Data Source Update and Dataset Refinement

The project initially used an **Amazon App Reviews dataset**, but the dataset focus has since changed to **Amazon product reviews**.

This required updates to the project documentation and data preparation pipeline.

The team explained that the documentation is currently being revised to reflect the new dataset and project direction.

---

## 9. API Access Challenges

The team attempted to use the **Amazon Product Advertising API** for additional product data.

However, they encountered several restrictions:

- Access requires creating an Amazon seller account.
- The account must have completed **sales within the last 14 days**.
- These requirements make it impractical for students who are not vendors.

Because of these constraints, the team decided not to pursue API-based enrichment.

---

## 10. Alternative Data Collection Attempts

The team explored other potential approaches to gather additional product information.

### SNAP Dataset

The SNAP Amazon dataset was considered but was found to be **too outdated** for the current analysis.

Additionally, category identifiers in the dataset did not match the categories used in the current dataset.

### Web Scraping

Web scraping was explored as a potential solution.

Ultimately, the team narrowed the dataset to **30 confirmed products** that matched the available review data.

---

## 11. Final Dataset Selection

The team refined the dataset significantly during the cleaning process.
Cleaning steps included:

- Removing duplicated reviews  
- Removing identical review text entries with the same timestamp  
- Removing empty review records  

The supervisor agreed that removing duplicates was necessary to prevent distortion in modelling results.

## 14. Hyperparameter Tuning Discussion

The team asked whether hyperparameter tuning should be included in the modelling process.

The supervisor advised that:

- Hyperparameter tuning is **not essential at the early stage**.
- The team should first implement **baseline models**.
- Tuning can be applied later to the **best-performing model** if time permits.

This approach helps avoid unnecessary complexity during early experimentation.

---

## 15. Model Explainability

The supervisor emphasized the importance of **model explainability** in the project.

In addition to predicting suspicious reviews, the model should ideally provide insight into:

- Which features influence predictions  
- Why a review was flagged as suspicious  

This would improve the interpretability of the system, especially in the final Streamlit application.

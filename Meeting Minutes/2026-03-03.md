## Summary of Discussion  

### 1. Sampling Strategy and Product Category Focus  

The supervisor suggested narrowing the scope of analysis instead of attempting to process the entire dataset.

**Key points:**

- Rather than analysing all products, the team could focus on one or two product categories (e.g., sports vs another product type).
- Sampling a representative subset (e.g., 8,000 reviews per day or per category) may still provide strong analytical insight.
- If certain enrichment approaches are not feasible, this should be clearly documented as a limitation.
- Creative constraints can still produce meaningful and informative results.

**Implication:**

Focusing on selected categories may allow clearer comparisons of suspicious behaviour across different product types.

---

### 2. External Dataset Integration (Kaggle Source)

A team member explored an additional dataset sourced from **Kaggle** containing approximately 1.4 million records.

**Key discussion points:**

- The dataset consists of separate product-related tables.
- A `LEFT JOIN` approach was attempted to match products with the main dataset.
- Out of approximately 15 million products, only 103,116 matched.
- Initial match rate was low but not insignificant.
- Some product names were manually cross-checked against Amazon listings for validation; while some matched the listed products, others did not.

**Supervisor feedback:**

- Low match rates should be documented.
- Matching quality may depend on product release/version differences.
- External joins may not significantly improve enrichment accuracy.
- Treat this as exploratory validation rather than guaranteed enhancement.

---

### 3. API-Based Enrichment Using Top Reviewed Products  

The supervisor suggested prioritizing API enrichment using highly reviewed products.

**Recommended approach:**

- Rank products by number of reviews.
- Start with the top 10 or top 50 most reviewed products.
- Use API allowance strategically to test feasibility.
- Sampling high-impact products may generate useful new features without needing full dataset coverage.

**Rationale:**

A small enriched subset could still provide valuable category-level features for modelling.

---

### 4. Duplicate Review Investigation  

A significant portion of the meeting focused on duplicate reviews.

**Findings:**

- Some reviews were repeated up to 12 times.
- In extreme cases, one review was duplicated over 200 times.
- Duplicates included:
  - Same reviewer ID  
  - Same review text  
  - Same product  
  - Same timestamp  

**In other cases:**

- Identical review text appeared across different timestamps.
- Some reviews appeared to be repeated months later with no textual changes.
- On actual product pages, only one instance of the review appeared.

**Interpretation:**

- Likely scraping artefacts or data pipeline issues.
- Some cases may represent system duplication rather than genuine user behaviour.
- These duplicates may have distorted earlier short-timeframe behaviour analysis.

**Decision:**

- Remove clear duplicates from the dataset.
- Document assumptions regarding scraping artefacts.
- Ensure data is properly cleaned before analysing reviewer behaviour.

**Supervisor guidance:**

If assumptions are made (e.g., treating duplicates as scraping errors), they must be clearly documented.

---

### 5. Data Cleaning Before Behavioural Analysis  

The team acknowledged:

- Reviewer burst patterns may have been inflated due to duplicates.
- Proper cleaning is necessary before drawing conclusions about suspicious activity.
- Analysis should not proceed on unverified or corrupted data.

**Agreed next steps:**

- Complete duplicate removal and cleaning.
- Re-run reviewer behaviour exploration after cleaning.

## Summary of Discussion

### 1. Data Enrichment Using Product Codes and APIs

The supervisor suggested enriching the dataset using product identifiers (e.g., product codes/ASINs).

**Key points:**

- Product codes can be searched online (e.g., on Amazon) to identify product details such as category and format (e.g., paperback books).
- The team was encouraged to explore whether APIs exist that allow automatic lookup of product information.
- Enriching the dataset with product category or type could introduce valuable new features for analysis.
- Automation is preferred over manual lookup due to dataset size.

**Notes:**

- Product type may influence review patterns.
- Adding a new column for product category could improve modeling.

---

### 2. Feature Selection for Suspicious Review Detection

The team discussed insights discovered during exploratory analysis:

- Verified purchase status alone is not a reliable indicator of authenticity.
- Some verified reviews showed suspicious patterns, including:
  - Large numbers of reviews posted in short timeframes
  - Extremely short reviews
- Time gaps between reviews (“diff time”) appear to be a useful signal.
- Review frequency patterns may indicate suspicious behavior.

The supervisor encouraged continued exploration to identify meaningful features.

---

### 3. Known Review Manipulation Practices

A potential scam pattern was discussed:

- Vendors may distribute products (or samples) to individuals in exchange for reviews.
- This can generate “verified purchase” labels while still producing biased reviews.

**Implication:**  
Multiple signals should be combined to detect suspicious activity rather than relying on verification status alone.

### 7. NLP Analysis and Text Processing

The team reported early findings from raw text exploration:

- Word cloud analysis showed more positive than negative words in reviews.
- This suggests an overall positive sentiment bias in the dataset.
- Data cleaning has not yet begun; analysis used raw text.

**Supervisor guidance:**

- Stop words (e.g., “the,” “and”) should be removed during preprocessing.
- Removing stop words is standard practice in NLP pipelines.

**Tools discussed:**

- NLTK for stop word removal
- spaCy as a modern alternative with customizable pipelines and LLM integration

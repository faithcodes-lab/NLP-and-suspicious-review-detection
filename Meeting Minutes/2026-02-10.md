## Summary of Discussion

### Dataset Selection and Suitability

The team presented a large-scale Amazon Alexa reviews dataset (100M+ records) containing review text, ratings, timestamps, user identifiers, and associated metadata (e.g., verified purchase indicators). The supervisor confirmed that the dataset is appropriate for the project, particularly due to its realistic scale and complexity, which aligns with industry-relevant data engineering and machine learning challenges.

### Research Scope: Definition of Fake / Deceptive Reviews

The supervisor emphasised the importance of clearly defining the concept of fake reviews within the project scope. Several categories were discussed, including:

Human-written deceptive reviews (e.g., incentivised or paid reviews)
AI-generated synthetic reviews
Copy-pasted or spam-like repetitive content
The team was advised to explicitly define the selected categories and provide academic justification within the project report.

### Labelling and Ground Truth Considerations

Existing labelled datasets from academic literature were discussed, including studies where participants generated deceptive reviews under controlled conditions (e.g., Amazon Mechanical Turk-based datasets). The supervisor recommended reviewing these studies in detail to understand their labelling methodologies, evaluation metrics, and transferable insights. 

### Feature Engineering and Behavioural Indicators

Potential linguistic and behavioural indicators of deceptive reviews were discussed, including:
Verified purchase status
Extreme rating behaviour (e.g., disproportionate 1-star or 5-star ratings)
Very short or generic review text
Temporal anomalies (e.g., multiple reviews posted within short time intervals)
Duplicate or highly similar review text (copy-paste detection)
Reviewer activity patterns over time
The supervisor cautioned against overgeneralisation and advised that all features must be empirically validated.

### AI-Generated Text and Copy-Paste Detection

The team proposed using AI detection tools to identify machine-generated reviews.. Copy-paste detection was highlighted as a promising and novel research direction for the project.

### Data Management and System Architecture

Given the dataset size, local processing using CSV files was identified as impractical. The supervisor adviced for further research on how to manage the data and recommended a database system (e.g., DuckDB) to enable scalable querying and efficient data handling. It was confirmed that Python can interface with these databases via appropriate connectors. Designing a collaborative data management architecture for the team was identified as a key technical component of the project.

### Project Scope and Risk Management

The supervisor advised limiting project scope to avoid overcomplexity. The team was encouraged to document research findings, datasets, and methodological decisions within GitLab to evidence individual contributions and support portfolio-based assessment.

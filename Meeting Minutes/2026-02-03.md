## Summary of Discussion

### 1. Review of Previous Tasks
- The team presented progress made following the previous week’s meeting with the lecturer.
- Tasks focused on identifying labeled datasets relevant to review analysis and examining accompanying research papers that describe labeling strategies.

### 2. Review of Identified Dataset
- A labeled dataset relevant to hotel reviews was identified and made available.
- However, the documentation explaining how the dataset was labeled was not sufficiently detailed or extensive.
- Concerns were raised regarding the reliability and transparency of the labeling process.

### 3. Review of App Reviews Dataset
- A research paper focusing on app reviews from the Google Play Store (across multiple applications) was reviewed.
- The paper provided a clear and well-documented labeling strategy for identifying fake and genuine reviews.
- The labeled dataset itself was not accessible, as the repository link was no longer functional.
- The authors of the paper were contacted, but no response had been received as of the meeting date (see Issue #5).

### 4. Discussion on Dataset Labeling
- Due to limitations in available datasets, the team discussed labeling a dataset independently.
- Multiple labeling approaches were considered.
- One proposed strategy by the supervisor involved using verified purchase indicators as a metric for distinguishing between genuine and fake reviews.
- The team explored the Amazon shopping website looking into revies from the  airfryer category to understand how verified purchase information could support labeling decisions.

### 5. Dataset Scope and Ethical Considerations
- The initial dataset explored focused on Amazon app reviews, while most publicly available labeled datasets are product-specific.
- The lecturer advised identifying a dataset that better aligns with the project objectives.
- Scraping data directly from Amazon was discussed and explicitly discouraged, as it violates Amazon’s terms and policies.

---

## Conclusions
- Existing datasets either lack sufficient documentation on ground truth labeling or are not accessible.
- A new dataset may be required to meet the project objectives.
- Any labeling strategy adopted must be well-documented, justified, and supported by academic literature.
